## Tier 4: use with caution

- Wikipedia (wikipedia.org): only for background, never as the source of a number, law or date.
- Law firm, accountant and appraiser blogs: no fixed list. Judge each one: who wrote it, is it dated, does it cite the law or ruling it describes. Prefer the primary source it points to.
- Professional associations, such as the Real Estate Appraisers Association or the Israel Builders Association: domains not confirmed yet. Use only after checking that the site is really the association's.

## Rules

1. **Freshness:** sources from the last 3 years. New projects, technology, prices, interest rates and market data must be much more recent: the last 12 months, and the most recent figure available. Laws and rulings can be older if they are still in force; check that they were not changed or cancelled.
2. **A fact needs the strongest source available.** A law, tax rate, regulation or official statistic comes from Tier 1. If only a news article reports it, say so in the report and try to find the official page.
3. **Never only Tier 4.** A fact found only in Tier 4 is marked `needs-check` and is not used unless the owner approves it.
4. **Numbers:** copy them exactly as the source gives them, with the date and what they measure (e.g. "average rent for a 3-room apartment in Tel Aviv, Q2 2026, per CBS"). Never round, estimate or combine numbers from different sources into a new one.
5. **Other sites:** allowed when the subject needs them (for example a foreign source for an international trend), but mark them `tier: other` and explain why they are trustworthy.
6. **Paywalls:** if a page cannot be read (TheMarker, Calcalist premium), don't guess its content from the headline. Find another source.
7. **Our own claims:** facts about Landlord Services (founding year, number of properties, services) come only from `content/site.ts`, never from the web.
