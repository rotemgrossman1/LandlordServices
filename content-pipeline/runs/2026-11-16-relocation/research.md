# Research: Relocation abroad: 5 things to take care of for your home and other properties in Israel

Run: content-pipeline/runs/2026-11-16-relocation/ · Researched: 2026-11-16

## Summary
- Leaving Israel does not end Israeli tax on the apartment: rental income must still be reported and taxed in Israel while you live abroad, and Israeli real estate stays under Israeli law. (F16, F23)
- Rental income has three tracks. The exemption ceiling is 5,654 ₪ a month in 2026 and drops to zero at 11,308 ₪. The 10% track (section 122) taxes gross rent with no expenses, and the tax is due within 30 days after the tax year ends. (F1, F2, F3, F6, F10)
- The exemption ceiling was frozen for three years in the 2025 budget. In November 2026 the Tax Authority is again pushing to tax rent from the first shekel and to create a rental registry. This is a proposal only, with "no deal" yet. (F7, F8, F9)
- Open question: can foreign residents use the exemption track? Older sources say yes, and 2022 proposals to deny it were never confirmed as enacted. Foreign residents do use the 10% track, but no Tier 1 page confirming either track for them could be opened. (F12, F13, F14, F15)
- Tax residency is judged by "centre of life", with rebuttable day-count presumptions (183 days, or 30 days plus 425 over three years). Tax residency and National Insurance residency are decided separately. Renting the home out on a long lease rather than keeping it available can affect the residency outcome. (F17, F18, F19, F20, F21)
- When you cut tax residency, an exit tax treats your capital assets as sold the day before you leave, or the tax can be deferred to an actual sale. (F22)
- Arnona: an empty-property discount exists (regulations allow up to 100% for 6 months, then less, up to 36 months), but Tel Aviv gives at most 6 months at 100%, once per ownership. The apartment must be empty of people and of furniture, and the discount runs only from the written notice. (F24, F25, F26, F27, F28)
- Lease under the Fair Rental Law (חוק שכירות הוגנת, a 2017 amendment to חוק השכירות והשאילה): the guarantee is capped at the lower of 3 months' rent or one-third of the whole term's rent. The landlord repairs within 30 days (3 days if urgent) and returns the guarantee within 60 days. (F29, F30, F31, F32, F33)
- Insurance: building cover is the owner's, contents cover is the tenant's. A long-vacant apartment can fall outside cover, and mortgage holders must have building insurance assigned to the bank. (F35, F36, F38, F39)

Exemption: eligible per sources from 2022 and 2002 (F13, F14); 2022 bill to deny it proposed for 1 Jan 2024, enactment not confirmed (F13). Not confirmed by any current Tier 1 page → needs-check

## Conflicts and gaps
- **Foreign residents and the exemption (open question).** S7 (Globes, 2022) and S9 (Ynet, 2002) say foreign residents qualify. S8 (JPost, June 2022) describes Bill 1555, which would deny it from 1 Jan 2024, and says the bill was "still being legislated". No source I could open says whether it passed. A search snippet (not a source) mentioned a later tax-reform draft limiting the exemption to Israeli residents from tax year 2024. Its status is also unknown. Treat foreign-resident eligibility for the exemption as **needs-check**. The article should not say either way until a Tax Authority or Kol Zchut page confirms it.
- **Foreign residents and the 10% track.** No Tier 1 page says outright that they qualify. S1 lists the conditions as "individual" plus "residential apartment in Israel", with no residency condition mentioned. S6 shows a foreign resident using 10% in practice. This is reasonably supported but not confirmed by Tier 1.
- **Exemption amount over time.** The amounts differ by year: 5,070 ₪ (S7, 2022 article), 5,196 ₪ for 2022 (S8) and 5,654 ₪ (2026: S2, S5; S4 gives the same 5,654 ₪ in Nov 2024). This fits the freeze reported in S3 (F7), but the freeze is single-source.
- **Pending change.** The first-shekel rental tax and rental registry (F8) are proposals for the upcoming Arrangements Law, not law. The article should present them as a possible change, dated 3 Nov 2026.
- **Blocked Tier 1 sources.** www.gov.il service and guide pages and www.kolzchut.org.il returned HTTP 403. www.tel-aviv.gov.il returned HTTP 472. These hosts were not reachable from this session (DNS not allowed): bat-yam.muni.il, cms-media.ramat-gan.muni.il, holders.tel-aviv.gov.il, embassies.gov.il, fs.knesset.gov.il, he.wikisource.org, en.globes.co.il, blogs.timesofisrael.com, nbn.org.il, and the law/accounting firm sites (efraty.com, bshcpa.co.il, capitax.co.il, menoralaw.com, asnafy.co.il, assets.kpmg.com). Only gov.il "BlobFolder" PDFs opened, which is how S1 was read. As a result, residency, arnona and Fair Rental facts rest on Tier 2 news, not on the official texts.
- **Power of attorney.** No current source on signing a POA at a consulate or with an apostille could be opened. F40 comes from a 2003 article. The consulate rules (bring an Israeli ID or passport, don't sign in advance) appeared only in search snippets, so they are not included.
- **Municipal change-of-holder procedure** (moving arnona into the tenant's name, documents required, what happens without written notice): not covered by any page I could open. Our own site describes this service (FAQ "utilities").
- **Vacant-apartment tax:** no source found on any separate tax on empty apartments in force today.
- **F34 (insurance cost)** is from a report on the bill as approved in committee (3 Jul 2017). S21 confirms that the law passed but does not mention this clause.
- **F35/F39** are "verified" from two pages by the same publisher (Bizportal), which is weaker than two independent publishers.
