# Research: ביטוח דירה למשכירים (home insurance for landlords in Israel)

Run: content-pipeline/runs/2026-11-23-landlord-insurance/ · Researched: 2026-11-23

## Summary
- In a rented apartment the cover is usually split. The owner insures the structure (מבנה) and the tenant insures their own belongings (תכולה). A tenant's contents policy costs about 300–700 NIS a year (F20, F21, F23).
- Owners should tell the insurer that the apartment is rented. A 2026 guide says not doing so is grounds for rejecting a claim. A 2016 article by an insurance lawyer says the result under the disclosure rules is a proportional cut in the payout, not a rejection. Either way, the owner loses money (F14, F15).
- The structure policy a bank requires for a mortgage covers the structure only. Contents, third-party liability and damage to the neighbours' property are not included, and the bank is the beneficiary up to the loan balance (F16, F17). Borrowers can buy this policy outside the bank (F18).
- Third-party liability and water damage (from the owner's own plumbing) are extensions to the standard policy, not basic cover. Since July 2015 the water extension also covers water that comes from a third party and blocked boilers and pipes (F6, F7, F8, F9). As of 2016, most public complaints about home insurance concerned water damage (F10).
- The structure's sum insured is based on the cost of rebuilding, not the market price, because land is not included (F4, F5). Sources disagree on underinsurance (ביטוח חסר). A 2016 official report says that since July 2015 insurers cannot cut payouts for partial damage to the structure. 2026 guides say there is a proportional cut once the gap reaches 15%, and give a water-damage example (F2, F3). See Conflicts.
- Without a waiver of subrogation (ויתור על שיבוב) toward the tenant, the owner's insurer can recover its payout from a tenant who caused the damage (F24, F25).
- The 2017 "fair rental" amendment put building insurance on the landlord. A tenant cannot be required to buy a structure policy, under section 25ט per ynet (F26, F27).
- The 2015 amendment extended the period of rent payments in the standard policy (F11). A 2022 draft proposed paying lost rental income for up to 24 months; whether it was adopted is unknown (F12). Private policies generally exclude war damage, which the state compensates (F28).

## Questions covered
- What does a landlord need to insure, and what is the tenant's part? → answered by F20, F21, F23, F26, F27
- Must I tell my insurer the apartment is rented, and what happens if I don't? → answered by F14, F15 (consequence disputed)
- Is the bank's mortgage structure insurance enough? → answered by F16, F17, F18, F19 (one publisher only)
- Am I covered if water from my apartment damages the neighbour, or if a tenant or guest is injured? → answered by F6, F7, F22, F23
- How is water damage covered, and how is it repaired? → answered by F7, F8, F9, F10, F29
- How much should the structure be insured for, and what is underinsurance? → answered by F2, F3, F4, F5 (conflict on how underinsurance applies)
- Can the insurer sue my tenant (subrogation)? → answered by F24, F25
- Am I covered for lost rent if the apartment becomes uninhabitable? → partly answered by F11, F12, F28. Not answered: the current maximum period or amount in the standard policy. The only figure found is in a 2022 draft (24 months, implying 12 months today), and insurer policy PDFs could not be opened.
- What did the 2017 rental law change on insurance? → answered by F26, F27 (statute text not read directly)
- Is an apartment left empty between tenants covered? → not answered: search snippets of insurer policies define "דירה שאינה תפוסה" as empty for more than 60 consecutive days with reduced cover, but the policy PDFs (harel-group.co.il, aig.co.il) were outside the allowed network and could not be read. Only the fact that the standard policy defines the term is confirmed, by S3, which inserts a new definition "after the definition of 'דירה שאינה תפוסה'".

## Conflicts and gaps
- **Underinsurance on partial structure damage (main conflict).** S1 (Tier 1, March 2016) says the July 2015 amendment means an insurer "לא תוכל להפחית את תגמולי הביטוח במקרה של נזק חלקי למבנה הדירה". S4/S5 (Bizportal, Aug 2026) apply a proportional cut to a 300 thousand NIS water-damage claim on an underinsured structure, above a 15% threshold. A reader comment under S5 (29/08/2026) also disputes the article. S1 is more authoritative, but I could not read the current regulation text (Nevo, Wikisource and insurer policy PDFs were outside the allowed network). The 15% threshold may apply to contents or total loss rather than to partial structure damage, but that is unconfirmed. Recommendation: build the "underinsurance" mistake around insuring for the rebuild cost (F4) and around total loss or contents. Do not use the 222k/78k water-damage example without the owner's approval.
- **Consequence of not telling the insurer that the apartment is rented.** S6 (2026) says "grounds for rejecting a claim"; S12 (2016, lawyer) says a proportional reduction. The underlying law (Insurance Contract Law sections 6–7, 17–18) could not be read: fs.knesset.gov.il and he.wikisource.org were unreachable. Safe wording: "it can cut or endanger the payout".
- **Furnished apartments.** S5 says a landlord buying contents cover for a rented apartment "pays for property that belongs to the tenant". But S4 defines contents as items owned by the insured, and S10 mentions landlords leaving furniture and appliances. The owner's own furniture and appliances in a furnished rental are therefore the owner's contents, and S5's statement is a simplification. Not confirmed by a policy text.
- **Rent loss.** The current maximum rent-loss period or amount in the standard policy was not found in a readable source. S1 only says it was extended in 2015. S3 (a 2022 draft) implies 12 months. A sponsored Ynet piece (2021, Phoenix) says 12 months but was not used. A gov.il service "הגשת תביעה לפיצוי עבור אובדן שכר דירה" (Tax Authority, war damage) exists, but the page returned 403. ## Angles for us
- Mistake: "the bank's policy is enough." The mortgage structure policy leaves out third party and the neighbours (F16, F17), which matters most in a rented apartment where a tenant's leak floods the flat below (F23). Links: `rental-management`; `/services#rental-management`.
- Mistake: "I didn't tell the insurer it's rented" and "no subrogation waiver" (F14, F15, F24). Both are things a lease with legal support (`rental-management` includes "חוזה שכירות עם ליווי משפטי") and `landlord-safe` ("הכנת חוזה השכירות") can handle. Links: `/landlord-safe`, `/services#landlord-safe`.
- Mistake: "insured for the market price, or for an old figure," i.e. underinsurance (F4, F5). Avoid the contested partial-damage example (see Conflicts). A neutral angle: check the sum insured against rebuild cost when each new lease starts.
- Mistake: "no water extension / no idea who claims what" (F7–F10, F23). This links to our handling of faults and tradespeople ("טיפול בכל התקלות... כולל תיאום בעלי מקצוע") under `rental-management`. Keep wording to coordinating the repair; there are no facts about us filing insurance claims.
- Owners abroad (`abroad`, `/services#abroad`): the split between owner structure cover and tenant contents cover (F20, F26, F27), and someone on site after water damage. Also relevant to `luxury` for furnished, high-value contents (see the furnished-apartment conflict).
- Rent loss: our Landlord Safe rent guarantee covers tenant non-payment, subject to its terms. It is not insurance for an uninhabitable apartment (F11, F12, F28). An article should not conflate the two. `site.ts` does not say Landlord Safe covers damage-related rent loss.
